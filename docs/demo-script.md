# Demo script (under 3 minutes)

The recording (`artifacts/synesthesia-demo.mp4`, made by `scripts/record-demo.mjs`) has no voice-over: chapter captions at the top name each part, and the app's own live caption in the dock narrates every note. Live, say the bold line of each part; the screen carries the numbers. Times are approximate and match the recording.

## 0. Landing (0:00–0:15)
**"Earth, heard. Three ways to listen to NASA measurements, and one bridge between them."** A hand across the Blue Marble plays the picture's brightness. Scroll past the three experience cards (Hear Earth, environmental history, global anomalies), the Listening Intelligence bridge, and the case study and Listening Test cards.

## 1. Earth Listening Intelligence: the Indian Ocean (0:15–0:50)
**"One click, and the Indian Ocean plays: twelve years of NASA POWER months in under a minute."** Click **Try the Indian Ocean**; the sound starts at once. Higher is warmer than that month's 2001–2020 normal; bells ring when temperature, rain, wind or humidity is unusual.
- **"The same numbers are searched for what was unusual."** The "What the measurements say" summary and the ranked findings: each is a sentence filled from computed numbers, not generated text.
- Click **Hear it** on a finding: the audio jumps to that month and the finding lights up. Open "The measurements behind this": the table, the grid points and the NASA POWER request URLs.

## 2. Hear Earth (0:50–1:05)
**"Any point on Earth, as a soundscape."** Cosmic Earth plays a place's NASA POWER days. Open **Trace**: the sounding note, back to its measurement.

## 3. Travel through environmental history (1:05–1:20)
**"A place since 1981, one note per step."** The Sound Timeline against the 2001–2020 normal, with the chart following the note.

## 4. Discover global anomalies through sound (1:20–1:35)
**"48 points around the globe; the outliers ring."** The Anomalies tab lands on the Global Anomaly Explorer. Scan, then hear the anomaly timeline.

## 5. Case study: four charts, or one sound (1:35–2:10)
**"Can you find a real event by ear?"** The page has searched the Amazon record for its strongest compound heat-and-dryness month. Play the quiet reference year, then the event year: the pitch climbs and the bells ring. **Reveal**: the template headline, four synced charts (temperature, rainfall, humidity, wind) with the event band and the playhead, and beside them the one soundscape that carries all four, with its legend.

## 6. Listening Test (2:10–2:35)
**"And can people hear it? We built the instrument to find out."** Two real 30-day clips, ear only: no place, dates, values or note names. Play clip 1 and clip 2; answer with 1 or 2. The protocol and the planned analysis are in `docs/validation-protocol.md`; the simulated observer is labelled as simulation, and human results will be filled after the study.

## 7. More (2:35–2:50)
**"Everything else supports these three."** Open **More**: case study, Listening Test, Science, then Sound Map, Time Machine, Sound Battle, Jukebox and Sonic Lab. Escape closes it; back to the start.
